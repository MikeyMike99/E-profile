# Recovery: The Long Road Back

Hey, I have another post for you regarding my recovery after my journey inside the hospital. I spent **three months** in the hospital getting treatment and was consulted by many therapists. During that time, I had to learn how to eat again and how to walk. While I could speak normally, my brain struggled to process incoming information; people had to speak very slowly for me to be able to process and understand them.

I was finally released, but only a week later, I was back. 

### The Midnight Scare
I woke up in the middle of the night—around 1:00 AM—to find my pillow completely soaked. My first guess was that it was blood. I still had stitches at the back of my head that had been removed just a day or two before.

The cut ran from the **crown of my head down to my neck**. A part of it had opened again at the very back. It wasn't blood, but a fluid buildup beneath my skin that had torn the soft healing tissue open.

I was admitted for another two or three weeks. The doctors:
* **Inserted drainage pipes** to try and stop the fluid from building up.
* **Operated again**, cutting the whole thing open to wash out the wound because they feared a deep infection.
* This was a major concern because I also have a **plate at the back of my skull**.

They stitched me back up and released me. The stitches were removed and things seemed fine for about a month, but then it opened up again. I didn’t go back to the doctors immediately that time; it wasn't a big hole, and I thought if I just gave it time, it would heal.

---

# Finding a House

While I was in the hospital the first time, barely functioning, I got news that the student home where I stayed (held by *Helpende Hand*) had been sold. I had to find a new place to stay while I was still recovering.

Thankfully, the retired head of the children's home where I grew up stepped in. She is the one who found me a place at an **old age home**. It was very convenient because I had food and a place to rest my head. During this time, I started attending **mobility training** two to three times a week to learn how to navigate the world and cross streets using a **white cane**.

By November, my girlfriend and I started talking about marriage and starting a family. I went out and bought her a ring—not a proposal ring yet, but a promise that I would never leave her side.

---

# Heartbreak

By December, I was still very unsure about life and how I would make a living with my current condition. My girlfriend and I agreed we would figure it out, but I felt I needed to speak to her parents.

I think there was a misunderstanding of my intentions, but I asked them if I could marry their daughter in the future now that I am blind. 

**They did not even hesitate; they straight up said no.**

The ring just turned into a Christmas gift (which she already knew about since she chose it herself). A week or two later, she told me she "needed a break." To me, those words in a relationship usually mean a breakup. I didn't want to be a burden; I told her I would leave her in peace and that I was never her responsibility.

I called my father to pick me up with everything I owned. I was mad and heartbroken. Just a month prior, we were promising to never leave each other's side no matter what.

---

# Standing Strong: 2022

Now it is the year 2022. I refused to give up. I arranged and applied for a course at **Optima College**. I wanted to learn how to use a computer as a blind person, so I took the **End-User Computing course**.

This was supposed to be a six-month course, but it turned into nine.

**I will tell you that story in the next one.**
