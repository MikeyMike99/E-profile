# The Flip 6 Journey

Buckle up. This is going to be a long one.

I am trying to fit years of struggle, isolation, and growth into a single post. I will skip some details, but I want to give you the real picture of how I got here.

How did this happen? It goes back to the hole at the back of my head. Yes, the one that opened up after surgery and kept draining. Let us start there.

### 2022: Optima College and the Hospital

In March 2022, I started an End-User Computing course at Optima College in Pretoria.

A few months later, I had to see a neurologist. The hole at the back of my head would not close. At first, the doctor did not believe me. My bushy hair was hiding the damage. He had to cut the hair away with a scalpel just to see it. Even then, he was not convinced it was actively draining.

He booked me in for observation. When they were finally convinced, they scheduled surgery to wash out the wound. They also removed the metal plate that was holding my skull together. I spent three weeks on drips and antibiotics. Then they released me.

Three days later, the wound was leaking heavily again. I was back in a hospital bed.

This is where things got terrible. The doctors focused entirely on the surface wound. They put me on an oxygen tank, only to get the wound healed. It felt like they were not listening to me.

Finally, I snapped. Only then did they start to look at the problem more closely. A severe infection had eaten completely through my skull bone. It was slowly making its way to my brain. They had to surgically drill the infected bone out of my head. How the surgeon did not notice the infection when they removed the plate is beyond me.

This is how the nasty ordeal ended. The wound healed normally, thankfully to this day.

I got back to Optima College. I managed to complete the end-user course. I am thankful they extended the course for the whole group due to my misfortune, but I rose stronger.

By December, my father returned from America and picked me up from the college. On Christmas, we were fully packed and loaded to move to St. Lucia. It was a stormy night, the drive was long and stressful. We made it, unpacked, and got ready for the next chapter to unfold.

# Deals Made and Broken

The reason we packed and moved to St. Lucia: My father's nephew bought a piece of land there. They made a deal that they would turn the property into a holiday suite of cabins.

The thought was nice, though never written down or formally planned out very well. In any case, during my stay at Optima, we tried to build a cabin for me and my father to stay in. By the time we arrived in St. Lucia, the cabin was not fully built. They spent two weeks finishing the cabin. We were basically sleeping outside with no protection. Luckily for us, nature was graceful to us. This is where I stayed until September 2023. My father left earlier in the year in April, back to America to make some more money.

### 2023: Left in the Dark in St. Lucia

Family relationships broke down not long after our arrival in St. Lucia, so I was completely alone in the cabin once my father left. I had no transport. Nobody to help me do the basic living life things, as you may call it. This was a very lonely and difficult time. I was simply waiting for my father to return. That did not happen. My father then decided he wanted to buy his own piece of land, so that is what he did. I had to make arrangements to pack everything up once again and get transport to the new property in the Free State. The property was registered in my name.  I managed to do all of it on my own. The new property did not have water. I only had a 3x3 wooden wendy house which I stayed in; the other was storage space. My father did arrange someone to stay with me on the property. It was a family of three. They bought themselves a caravan, packed their stuff, and left their own troubles behind for a better future, which turned out not to be the best option for them. They only lasted four months until our troubles became too much for them. I did not want them to do what they did. I did not want them to offer up their safe haven, but the deal for them was too good to turn down. My father offered them the opportunity to build their own house on the property free of charge, never to pay rent, just because of their helping hand. Again, I am not going into details here. It just did not work out.


When they left. My father  made the same deal again with another family member. The cycle just repeated. Problems started to came up. This time I am the centre point. Me and my father is not on speaking terms anymore the relationship is broken, he is still in America has not returnd yet. 
### 2024: The Void and the Screen Reader

I needed an escape. I turned to audio games.

My friends and I played a game called Survive the Wild (STW). We explored and did quests using our screen readers. But eventually, the developer shut it down.

Sighted people have thousands of games to choose from. We had almost nothing. I tried Euro Fly Simulator 3, the simulator for the blind. This was boring. You would fly in a straight line for hours, lose focus, crash, and waste your day. I heard the blind community on Discord complaining about the lack of good games.

# My Breaking Point

October 2024. I decided I was done waiting. I was going to build a game myself. I simply asked ChatGPT to create me a game. It came up with an open world where the player can walk and turn and jump. I thought this was easy, just add some sound. I thought this is the way forward: just ask the AI to give me snippets of code, make it work, and there I go. I do not have to learn code. I do not really have to know how it works as long as it works, but it is not that simple. I quickly realized that I needed to learn Python. So that is what I did.

I started learning Python. It was a massive wall. I first started with a terminal game, but this was terrible. The screen reader did not want to cooperate; debugging was hell. So I moved to interfaces. I set up an interface, but now nothing, no output. These interfaces do not have screen reader support built in. I tried building game windows with Pygame. My screen reader, NVDA, gave me nothing. Imagine trying to build a world you cannot see, and your only tool refuses to speak to you. It was pure frustration. Tkinter and Pandas were the same. Just dead silence.

I kept pushing. I searched for answers. Finally, I found a custom NVDA DLL file. I plugged it in, and suddenly, my code spoke back to me.

I was no longer trapped in the terminal. I could build real, interactive audio environments. I programmed player movement and spatial audio cues. Every time my game crashed, I read the error logs line by line and fixed it. For the first time in a long time, I was taking control. I am just moving forward from there, not much because of my studies. Debugging takes time which I simply cannot offer up at this time.

### 2025: The Leap to Cape Town and Cyber Security

In late 2024, I went back to Optima College for a call centre course. It was easy, but it felt like it does not hold real value for making a life out of it. I wanted a real future.

In January 2025, I took a massive risk. I contacted Hein Wagner Academy. They asked how quickly I could get to Cape Town. I told them: "Sooner than soon."

Three days later, I packed up my life. I moved across the country.

I dropped the call centre course and enrolled in their Business Marketing program. I originally wanted to study Cyber Security, but that program was not open yet. I used the marketing course to prove I belonged there.

The course was brutal. It required intense studying and passing strict exams. I fought through every tough test. I proved my willingness to adapt and work hard.

I passed. I now hold that N4 Certificate in my hands. Feeling the physical paper of that certificate meant everything. It proved I was not just a patient in a hospital bed, or a lonely guy in a cabin anymore. I had earned my place.

Because of my high marks, the academy saw my drive. They officially sponsored me for their full three-year Cyber Security program.

That is where I am today. My cyber security journey is my absolute priority. My audio game development comes right after. I left my old rabbit farming and side projects behind. No, wait, not left behind. I will simply find a better time to pursue this part of my life again. I love nature and the outside world. Currently, and for the past few years since I became blind, computers are the only thing that keeps me occupied effectively. Technology is the future; I want to master it. I want to make people aware that blind people can actually 
uttilise technology and contribute to society. I want to show the world that blind people has no limits. The only limit they have is to get from point A to point B with no form of guidance in the physicle world.
