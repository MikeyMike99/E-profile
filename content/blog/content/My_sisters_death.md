# The Day Everything Changed: My sisters Death
I’ve been putting this off for a long time.
Writing about my sister’s passing isn’t something that comes easily, but I promised I would share this part of my life. If you’re sensitive to stories about death, this might not be the post for you.
But this is my story, and today I’m finally ready to tell it.
 
## A Good Memory Before the Darkness

It was 15 February 2017.
Before I talk about what happened, I want to hold on to the last happy memory I have with her.
The day before felt normal. I went to work, came home, and later my sister and I visited my girlfriend at the time. Nothing special — just a simple, warm evening.
The three of us took a small joyride.
Windows down.
Music loud.
My sister in the backseat acting like the official DJ.
She kept replaying the worship song “One Way.”
Then I took over and played “Suggustief” by Jan Blou Kaas — her favourite.
She loved that song.
To this day, I can still listen to “Suggustief.”
But when “One Way” plays… I can not keep the flood gates closed...
We ended the evening laughing, joking, and enjoying life.
I told her I’d take her back to the institute the next evening around six.
And just like that, we went to bed — not knowing that was the last peaceful moment we would ever share.
 
## The Morning That disaster struck
My alarm went off the next morning.
I felt heavy, unmotivated, almost like something inside me didn’t want to go to work.
I even thought about calling in sick.
But I pushed myself up, showered, and got ready.
My sister was awake.
I told her to pack her things, and to put anything she wasn’t taking into the cupboard so she could find it easily next time she visited me.
I left the gate remote with her as usual, since I walked two blocks to catch a lift with my colleague.
Nothing seemed wrong.
Nothing felt out of place.
Work was the same as always.
I walked in, headed straight for the kitchen, and started making coffee.
Then my phone rang.
 
## The Call You Never Want to Get
I lived in a student house with a few other people. That day, the gardener and cleaning lady were scheduled to come in.
The gardener arrived early and walked past the living room window — the one without curtains.
He saw my sister lying on a blanket on the floor.
To him, she looked like she was just sleeping.
He told the students in the pool house, and one of them called me.
All she said was:
“You need to come home. Something’s going on with your sister.”
I asked if it was serious. She was already crying.
I asked if it was something where my sister needed medical help.
Her reply:
“I think it’s past that.”
I didn’t know what that meant.
I only knew it felt wrong.
I ran around work telling everyone I had to leave immediately.
I got a lift back — stuck in the worst morning traffic.
Every robot red.
I was shaking, sweating, panicking.
My colleague drove normally.
He had no idea what kind of nightmare we were heading into.
I phoned my girlfriend and asked her to go to my house.
 
## The Moment of truth
When we finally arrived, I saw the ambulance in the yard.
My heart dropped.
Inside, the paramedics were crouched over my sister with heart monitors and equipment.
I stood there, confused, scared, unable to understand what I was seeing.
After a minute or two, one paramedic looked at the other and quietly asked:
“Should we call it...?”
He checked the time.
The other paramedic just nodded his head with a helpless expression.
They turned to me and told me the words I will never forget:
“It’s too late.”
No explanation.
No warnings.
No signs before this morning.
Just… gone.
That was how my sister left this world.
 
Breaking the News
The first person I called was my father.
I told him:
“If you’re driving, pull off the road.”
He wasn’t driving. He asked what happened.
I told him his daughter had passed away.
He didn’t believe me at first, but he heard the truth in my voice.
I went outside, fell into my girlfriend’s arms, and my legs gave in.
I was shaking.
Everything inside me was breaking at once.
We waited three hours for the mortuary to come and pick up her body.
The autopsy found nothing.
Her death certificate simply said:
Cause of death: Under investigation.
 
The Week That Didn’t Feel Real
The next three days were a blur.
I had to organise everything.
Coffins were far more expensive than I ever imagined.
My girlfriend knew someone who could give me a discount, but I still couldn’t afford it.
The children’s home stepped in and paid for it.
My father wanted her buried in our family graveyard — the same one our family has used since the 1800s.
It was far from where I lived, but I agreed.
The mortuary charged too much for transport, so we transported her ourselves.
Then came the struggle to buy a grave.
Because she wasn’t from that town, the price was higher.
We had no money left and had to borrow.
Everything took about a week and a half.
My grandfather, being a pastor, did the service.
 
## What We Still Don’t Know
To this day, we still don’t know why my sister passed.
We asked questions, we searched for answers, but eventually we had to stop.
Nothing they could tell us would bring her back.
Knowing or not knowing wouldn’t change anything.
It wouldn’t take away the pain, and it wouldn’t make the outcome different.
What matters is that there was nothing criminal involved,
and it wasn’t a suicide.
She simply slipped away without warning, and we had to accept that some things remain unanswered.
 
Life Doesn’t Pause for Grief
When everything was finally over, I returned to work.
Life around me carried on like nothing happened.
People said “sorry,” and then moved on with their day.
But honestly, I don’t think there’s anything anyone can say to make that kind of pain disappear.
Part of me felt a strange peace — not because she was gone, but because she didn’t have to struggle anymore.
She went peacefully, and that thought is something I hold on to.
On the day of her burial, my grandfather told me something that never left my mind:
“Nothing is as uncertain as death.”
 
Closing This Chapter
Writing this post brought back every memory, every tear, every moment from that day.
It took me a long time to put all of this into words.
But it’s part of my journey, and I want to share it honestly.
Next time, I’ll tell you the story of how I became blind.
Until then.
