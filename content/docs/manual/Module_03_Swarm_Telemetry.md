# Module 3: Swarm Telemetry & Metadata Logging

## What Will I Learn in this Module?
This module establishes how Tier 5 Admins monitor the massive data flow generated by an active AI Swarm. You will learn:
* **Swarm Telemetry:** The fundamental principles of tracking AI communication across WebSockets.
* **The Black Box Effect:** Why decentralized AIs are incredibly difficult to audit, and how to pierce the veil.
* **TokenDumps vs SwarmFlow:** The difference between full transcript captures (payload recording) and flow-based metadata logging.
* **Chrono-Syncing (NTP):** Why precise timestamp synchronization is critical for tracking Agent-to-Agent Worms.

## 3.1 Swarm Telemetry & The Black Box Effect
When an organization unleashes a swarm of autonomous AI Subagents to manage its game assets, it inevitably encounters the **Black Box Effect**. 

Because modern Large Language Models operate non-deterministically, they do not produce the exact same output every time. If a Subagent suddenly deletes a crucial database table, a standard system log will only tell you *that* it was deleted, not *why* the AI decided to do it. The internal "thought process" of the AI is a black box.

To pierce this black box, the Siraugga Engine relies on **Swarm Telemetry**. Telemetry is the continuous, automated collection of data from remote Subagents, routed back to the Orchestrator for analysis. Without rigorous telemetry, a rogue Agentic Worm could infect the swarm, moving laterally from one `/playground` to another, while Tier 5 Admins remain entirely blind to the attack.

## 3.2 TokenDumps (Full Transcript Captures)
In traditional networking, a Packet Capture (PCAP) records the entire contents of a network packet. In DevCore, the equivalent is a **TokenDump** (or Full Transcript Capture). 

A TokenDump records every single token generated by the AI, including its hidden chain-of-thought, its exact tool payloads, and the raw system prompts. These are stored in massive, un-truncated `transcript_full.jsonl` files deep in the Orchestrator's brain directory. 

While TokenDumps are the ultimate forensic tool—allowing an Admin to read the exact hallucination that led an AI to execute a malicious command—they are incredibly expensive to store. Recording the full transcript of a 100-Agent swarm generating millions of tokens a minute will instantly exhaust server quotas. Therefore, TokenDumps are typically reserved for deep forensic investigations after a breach has already occurred, rather than real-time monitoring.


## 3.3 SwarmFlow & Agentic 5-Tuples (Metadata Logging)
Because storing Full Transcript Captures is incredibly storage-intensive, the Orchestrator primarily relies on **SwarmFlow** (DevCore's equivalent to Cisco NetFlow or IPFIX).

SwarmFlow does not record the actual words or JSON payloads the AI generates. Instead, it records the lightweight *metadata* of the conversation. To do this, it tracks the **Agentic 5-Tuples**:
1.  **Modder ID:** The original human user who triggered the session.
2.  **Parent Agent ID:** The specific Subagent making the request.
3.  **Target Namespace:** The directory, file, or tool being accessed.
4.  **Model Tier:** The LLM engine used (e.g., Flash, Pro).
5.  **Token Volume:** The total tokens billed during the interaction.

By analyzing SwarmFlow databases, Tier 5 Admins can identify peak usage times, detect Denial-of-Service attacks (e.g., a Modder looping the AI to drain tokens), and track the movement of a Prompt Injection as it hops from Subagent to Subagent, all without having to read massive transcript files.

## 3.4 Chrono-Syncing the Swarm (NTP)
In a massive decentralized Swarm, fifty different Subagents might be executing tasks simultaneously across fifty different sandboxed containers. If an Agentic Worm spreads through the system, a Tier 5 Admin must be able to piece together the exact order of events across all fifty containers to trace patient zero.

This is mathematically impossible if the system clocks of the individual sandboxes are out of sync by even a fraction of a second.

To solve this, the Orchestrator enforces **Chrono-Syncing** (utilizing a rigid Network Time Protocol). Every single log generated by a Subagent, whether it is a Sandbox Event Log or a SwarmFlow metadata record, is immediately stamped with a perfectly synchronized UTC timestamp before being forwarded to the central logging server. This ensures that the chronological narrative of a cyberattack remains undeniably accurate during an audit.
